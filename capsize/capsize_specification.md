# Game Specification: The Capsize Engine

> **Status:** Working single-file prototype at `capsize/index.html`. Forked directly from `weather/index.html` (see that folder's spec for the original "Weather Balance Engine" this was built from) to answer the question: how hard is it to reuse the same vector-differential core for a different metaphor? Answer: cheap for the mechanics, a real rendering rewrite for the visuals. This doc describes what actually got built.

## 1. Concept
The player crews a ship in an endless storm. A 2D tilt vector — **List** (port/starboard roll) and **Trim** (bow/stern pitch) — is pushed around by ballast weight, restorative hull righting force, and random wave/wind noise. Crossing a capsize-angle boundary ends the voyage. Same core philosophy as the weather prototype: **you are not trying to hold the ship level. You are trying to keep the rocking survivable.**

## 2. What ported over unchanged
* The fixed-timestep tick loop, the force/friction/noise vector equation, the `threshold`-crossing capsize/reset lifecycle, and the permanent-bonus prestige math are structurally identical to `weather/index.html` — only renamed (`climateEngine` stays the same object shape, `factions` → four stations, `pop` → ballast, `devotion` → crew stamina).
* The Rally-style delayed/imprecise conversion action ported over almost verbatim as **Ballast Trim**: click a station's card, pay stamina up front, wait `RALLY_LAG_TICKS = 3`, then a randomized `RALLY_MIN`–`RALLY_MAX` (8–20) headcount gets pulled unevenly from the other three stations (never below `RALLY_MIN_LEFT = 5`). This mechanic turned out to fit a ship even better than it fit weather-deity prayer — "the crew can't move heavy cargo precisely on a pitching deck" is a more intuitive justification for sloppy/delayed action than the original's political-imprecision framing.
* Click-the-population-bar-card interaction (no separate button grid) — carried over from the last round of weather iteration.

## 3. What changed, and why

### The growth/feedback loop is now symmetric, not asymmetric
The original spec had exactly two of four factions (Rain, Sunshine) with a triggered exponential growth path, chosen for thematic "prayer" reasons that don't map onto a boat. Here, **every station floods when the ship is currently leaning toward it** — `alignment = dot(W, station.dir) / threshold`; positive alignment compounds that station's ballast (`FLOOD_GROWTH`), matching the textbook "list → water pools to the low side → list worsens" capsize spiral. This is:
* More physically honest for the metaphor (it's *the* standard explanation of how boats actually capsize).
* Simpler code — one loop over all four stations instead of two special-cased branches plus two baseline-growth branches.
* Slightly faster-paced than the weather prototype at equal constants, because up to four directions can compound instead of two. Retuned `FLOOD_GROWTH` down from the weather prototype's `0.020` to **`0.014`** (validated by headless Node simulation) to land back in a comparable ~350–680 tick unmanaged-crash window.

### The visual layer is a real rewrite, not a reskin
This is the part that isn't "free." The weather prototype's presentation is a *global color/particle blend* driven by which direction the vector points (an abstract "the whole island feels sunny" idea). A boat needs to **physically tilt** — that's the whole point of a capsize game — so the rendering approach had to change:
* The vector's `x`/`y` components directly rotate (`roll`, up to `MAX_ROLL_RAD ≈ 24°`) and vertically shear (`pitch`, via a per-vertex offset scaled by each hull point's bow/stern position) a fixed 10-point hull polygon, drawn in local coordinates and transformed with `ctx.translate`/`ctx.rotate` — the ship visibly rocks in real time instead of the scene tinting.
* Replaced the organic sine-jittered island coastline with a **fixed hull silhouette** — a manufactured object doesn't need randomized terrain, so this is simpler code than the island's outline generation, not more.
* Dropped the four direction-gated elemental effects (rain/snow/gusts/bright-light) in favor of **one continuous storm** whose intensity (rain density, gust strength, lightning frequency, screen-edge danger vignette) scales with overall danger ratio (`|W|/threshold`) rather than vector direction — because "it is always storming" is the premise, not a state the vector drifts into.
* Added tilt-reactive detail that has no equivalent in the weather version: a waterline "wash" shape that climbs over whichever rail is currently low, and a bow-spray particle burst that kicks up when pitch crosses a threshold (the bow is visibly diving into the swell).
* The compass inset became an **inclinometer** — same code, relabeled, because a precise heel/trim gauge is an authentic real-world ship instrument, unlike the original's more abstract climate-radar framing.
* Nautical detail worth keeping in mind if this gets iterated further: station accent colors follow real navigation-light convention — **port = red, starboard = green** — which was a nice free authenticity win from reusing the existing four-color palette.

## 4. Verification
Same two-pass approach as the weather prototype: a physics-only headless Node simulation to tune `FLOOD_GROWTH` before writing any rendering code, then a DOM/canvas mock harness that `new Function()`-evaluates the real `<script>` block against fake `document`/`canvas`/`requestAnimationFrame` objects and drives the actual fixed-timestep loop for hundreds of frames. Confirmed: boots clean, all render paths fire without throwing (hull, rain, gusts, inclinometer, danger vignette), Ballast Trim queues/resolves with the expected sloppy math, and the capsize → banner → "Launch Next Hull" → reset lifecycle works end to end. Now also driven with a headless Playwright Chromium session against a local static server, confirming zero console errors and that the wheel, cannon battery panel, firing-arc indicator, and enemy raiders all actually render (screenshots taken across boot, a held rudder turn, a full reload→prime→aim→fire cycle, and a raider closing to engage range).

## 5. Helm, raiders, and cannons (session addition)
The original build was pure trim management with no way to actively steer or fight back. Added three tied-together systems:

* **Helm.** A new `heading` (yaw) state, independent of the existing list/trim vector, driven by a `rudder` value that eases toward held wheel input (`RUDDER_EASE_RATE`) rather than snapping — the wheel has weight. Turning isn't free: a deflected rudder feeds `-rudder * TURN_HEEL_FORCE` into the same `vector.x` the storm pushes on, so a hard turn heels the ship same as a rogue wave would. The wheel itself is a CSS-drawn graphic (rotating spokes) fed by `rudder`, next to hold-to-turn Port/Starboard buttons — no drag handling needed for a first pass.
* **Enemy raiders.** Tracked in ship-relative polar coordinates (`relBearing`/`distance`) rather than a world map, so the whole "radar" convention (ship fixed, world sweeps around it) extends naturally: turning the ship shifts every raider's `relBearing` by `-headingDelta` each tick. Raiders close to `ENGAGE_DISTANCE`, then hunt for whichever of the two broadside angles (90°/270°, i.e. abeam) is closer and drift toward it, firing on a per-tick chance once they're within `ENEMY_FIRE_ARC` of it. A hit chips `hullIntegrity` (a new resource, separate from the capsize threshold) and — the same trick used for cannon recoil — adds a random-direction instantaneous kick into the tilt vector, because a cannonball impact rocks a ship exactly like a wave does. Hull integrity hitting 0 ends the voyage via the same `endVoyage()` path as capsizing, just with its own banner text ("SHE'S GONE DOWN" vs. "SHE'S GONE OVER") — two distinct ways to lose, one shared lifecycle.
* **Cannons.** Two batteries (`port`/`starboard`) step through `empty → loading → loaded → priming → primed → ready → (fire) → empty`. Reload and Prime cost stamina and take a few ticks each (same "the crew needs a moment" logic as Ballast Trim's lag); Aim is instant but only succeeds if a live raider sits within that battery's arc and range, locking a `targetId`; Fire is instant, deals damage to the locked target if it's still valid, and — this was the specific ask that tied the whole feature together — kicks `vector.x` by `CANNON_RECOIL_FORCE` in the direction opposite the guns. Firing to starboard heels you to port. This is deliberately the same shape as the enemy hit and the rudder heel: every new system feeds the one vector the player has always been managing, rather than adding a parallel thing to watch.
* Net effect on the loop the user described: reload/prime/aim/fire adds four more things to juggle, but the reason it doesn't just feel like more busywork bolted on is that aiming requires being turned broadside (ties in the wheel) and firing costs you trim (ties into the core capsize mechanic) — "keep trim, rotate for broadsides, fire the cannons" ends up being one interconnected balancing act, not three separate systems running in parallel.

## 6. Open threads
* **Balance is unvalidated against a human**, now more so than before — the enemy/cannon constants (`ENEMY_HIT_DAMAGE`, `ENEMY_FIRE_CHANCE`, `CANNON_RECOIL_FORCE`, arc widths) were picked for "seems reasonable" pacing, not tuned against anyone actually trying to hold trim, turn broadside, and time a shot at once. This is the single biggest thing to playtest next.
* **The rocking transform is 2D pseudo-perspective**, not real. At extreme roll/pitch values it may look more like the hull is stretching than rotating in 3D — worth an eyeball pass, and if it reads wrong, a cheaper fix than true 3D is probably just clamping `MAX_ROLL_RAD` lower and leaning harder on the waterline-wash/vignette cues to sell danger instead.
* **The wheel doesn't visually reflect heading itself**, only rudder deflection — the ship graphic always points "up" regardless of `heading` (by design, so raiders/rendering stay in the simpler ship-relative frame), but that means `heading` is currently a readout number with no other gameplay hook. It's plumbing for a future "sail toward a destination while surviving the storm" mode more than something the current fight-in-place loop needs yet.
* **No enemy return-fire counterplay beyond passive tanking** — right now the only response to an incoming raider is racing your own reload/prime/aim/fire cycle or just outlasting it; there's no maneuver (e.g. presenting a narrower silhouette bow-on) that reduces incoming hit chance.
* **No sound** — a storm-and-ship setting is arguably a stronger candidate for audio investment (creaking hull, wave crash, thunder, cannon fire) than the weather prototype was, since the whole fantasy is bodily/physical rather than atmospheric.
* **"Fog navigation" is still unbuilt.** This prototype answers the *capsizing* (and now *combat*) half of the original question; the fog/wayfinding idea was assessed as a genuinely different game (destination-seeking under limited visibility, not a wobble-to-manage) and hasn't been started.
